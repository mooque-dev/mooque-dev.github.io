Cancer Care Dashboards, Revisited

I designed dashboards for MyJourney, a cancer-care platform for clinicians, staff and patients. This is what I would have done differently, knowing what I know now.

Role
UX/UI Designer
Client
MyJourney
Team
UX researcher, product manager, engineers
Tools
Figma
4screens rebuilt as working, interactive redesigns
4mistakes that repeat across both products, fixed once in the system
2products: a patient-tracking dashboard and a cancer-conference tool
Stakes
MyJourney was founded by breast cancer surgeon Dr. Fahima Osman to guide patients from diagnosis through survivorship. The danger in that work is a quiet gap: a patient waiting between steps, or a case that never reaches the conference.
Tension
Clinicians and staff needed the whole picture and an answer to "who needs us today?" Dense tables and colorful charts gave the first and buried the second.
Easy path
Fix each screen as it came up. That is roughly what I did.
The call
Today I would lead each screen with the question it answers, and fix the repeating mistakes once, in the system.
Result
My redesign was never tested; funding ran out first. Below is how I would measure it, against the live tool’s published usability score as the bar to beat.

    05The pattern

    Four mistakes, two products

    One at a time, these look like small slips. Together, they are a system problem, and that is where I would fix them.

    PatternCare pathway toolConference toolSystem fix
    Unlabeled fractions"10/12" on stage cards"75/96" on session countsA count component that won't render without a label for its denominator
    Filters that don't filterStatus labels with no stated ruleDate range, quarter and cancer type disagree with the data shownOne filter state every panel reads, echoed in each title
    The same data twiceDonut and cards disagree on the totalBar chart repeats the table; attendees and rows repeatOne source per number, de-duplicated before it reaches the screen
    Color without meaningSix rainbow stage huesFive line hues, random avatar colorsColor only when it encodes something: one ramp for lateness, one accent for focus
    A headline number must reconcile.One mismatch costs the whole screen its credibility.
    Show time as time.If lateness is the problem, lateness is the shape.
    Never let color be the only signal.If urgency can't be read in grayscale, someone will miss it.
    Fix the pattern, not the screen.A mistake that repeats belongs in the system.

    What was measured, and what I'd measure next

    Mean System Usability Scale score, out of 100. 68 is the commonly cited average.

    Source: Herrington, Osman, Kastner et al., JMIR Cancer. Four staff participants across two clinics; the study evaluated the platform's clinical navigation tool, not this redesign.

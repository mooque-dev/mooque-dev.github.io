02 · Trust, safety and quality

What a payments team protects, shown in work that shipped.

  1. Trust

    Show the work, not just the answer.

    On Aplos × Keela I showed every data mapping and the sync status instead of hiding them behind defaults, because finance teams had been burned by silent syncs.

    Aplos × Keela

  2. Safety

    Build confidence before capability.

    The top fear was emailing all 5,000 donors by accident, so a preview of exactly who a workflow will touch comes before anything goes live.

    Automation

  3. Quality

    Surface errors while they are cheap.

    On Transaction Workflows, validation runs continuously, so a miscategorized transaction shows up now instead of at the final report.

    Transaction Workflows

03 · One decision, up close

Design the decision, not the default.

A new fee box had lifted opt-in to 82%, and everyone wanted to ship it. I asked the team to pause: donors who clicked Edit were being nudged to the minimum. We tested three fixes against the control, and the slider reached 92% with conversion steady.

A white-labelled rebuild of the checkout, after my MagicPatterns prototype. Pick a fallback, click Edit on the fee, and compare what you chose with what donors did.

04 · How I work

You know two circles that overlap. Most of my work happens in the overlap.

Design and Product

I write the hypothesis with the PM. When a fee change looked like a win, I argued to pause, tested four variations, and shipped the one that reached 92%.

Fee Opt-In Password

What a normal week looked like

  • Sitting with accountants while they reconciled, until their words made sense to me.
  • Drawing how data really moves between systems, so engineers and I argued about the same picture.
  • Agreeing with the PM and engineers on what we would not touch yet.
  • Prototyping in code when a static mock couldn't answer the question.
  • Rewriting my own design-system rules when the first version made people stop contributing.

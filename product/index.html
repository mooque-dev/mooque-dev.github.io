Aplos (now Velora) · 2025

Fix the feature behind the churn, without a rewrite

Problem
Nonprofits could record only one type per payment, but a gala ticket is part donation, part value received. Staff logged several records, receipts went wrong, and it was a top churn driver.
Evidence
56% of newly onboarded organizations voted for it, about 500 support tickets a quarter traced back to it, and 8 accountants I sat with all kept a parallel spreadsheet.
The call
Before designing, the PM, the engineering lead and I agreed what we couldn't do: no backend refactor, nothing that broke reporting. I pushed for a split that would extend to the next case, delivered in phases behind feature flags.
Result
Manual adjustments fell 60% and the work supported $1.8M ARR in connected accounts, compliant across Canadian, US and Australian tax rules.

Aplos (now Velora) · Raisely · 2026

Hold a win that was hiding a leak

Problem
A redesigned fee box lifted opt-in from 75% to 82%. But the 12 to 20% of donors who clicked Edit landed in an older box that nudged them to the smallest fee.
Evidence
Behaviour in that fallback, a 90% industry benchmark, and the fact that the leak sat exactly where donors change their mind.
The call
I made the case to pause the rollout instead of shipping the 82%. I set the hypothesis and the guardrails (conversion and average gift must hold), then tested three variants against the control in Pendo, prototyped in code so the team could adjust the logic first.
Result
The slider reached 92% with conversion and average gift steady, and shipped platform-wide the day the test closed. Mobile, where 60% of giving happens, is next.

Keela · 2021

Find the real obstacle before building the feature

Problem
Automation was the most requested feature and competitors were winning deals on it. It was first scoped at three weeks.
Evidence
Fifteen interviews with operations managers. The top fear wasn't complexity: “What if I accidentally email all 5,000 of our donors?”
The call
With the PM and the lead designer, I made the case for more time to research and test. We set confidence as the goal, so preview mode came first, and moved some user stories to a second release to hold the date.
Result
Three rounds of testing, each turned into prioritized work. It shipped as a paid add-on in about four months.

+13%

ARR, with 30% of 355 organizations using it

ARND · 2025 to now · founder

Owning a product end to end, on my own

A map-first app for Toronto’s small live shows. I did the research with fans and performers, set the priorities, built it with AI coding tools and run it in production. It’s in public beta, and the revenue plan is written, not live.

Curated listings over scraping
Small venues post shows in a dozen places, and scraped data is wrong often enough to break trust. I chose a curated, community-submitted catalog for the beta, even though it grows more slowly.
A staged switch to live data
The live catalog went behind a flag, so the app could fall back to known-good data while the real one proved itself.
An outage turned into a practice
When the catalog went down behind a green health check, I added monitoring on three surfaces and honest error states, not just a fix.

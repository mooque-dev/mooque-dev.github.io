<p>I designed the setup so customers configure the sync themselves, and every data mapping is visible before anything moves. Most integrations hide this behind defaults.</p>
<p>The faster option was to map everything automatically. We didn't build it. Each default mapping is a guess about where a field belongs, and in accounting a silent wrong guess is the worst outcome. So defaults cover only the clear cases, every mapping is shown and editable, and an exceptions list surfaces anything that went wrong.</p>
<div class="tablewrap"><table class="changes"><thead><tr><th></th><th>The manual workaround</th><th>The integration</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><span class="n">1</span></td><td data-label="The manual workaround"><b>Spreadsheets, CSV exports and duplicate entry,</b> because teams didn't trust what they couldn't see.</td><td data-label="The integration"><b>Visible data mapping.</b> Every Keela field shown against the Aplos account it lands in, and editable.</td></tr><tr><td><span class="n">2</span></td><td data-label="The manual workaround"><b>All or nothing.</b> Either map everything by hand or trust a black box.</td><td data-label="The integration"><b>Progressive configuration.</b> Defaults that cover about 80% of cases, with one click to go deeper.</td></tr><tr><td><span class="n">3</span></td><td data-label="The manual workaround"><b>"Did my data actually sync?"</b> No way to tell without checking by hand.</td><td data-label="The integration"><b>A sync status view.</b> Last sync time, records processed, and the exceptions that need attention.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>The work between the teams mattered as much as the screens. I ran the first joint design reviews between the two engineering teams, built the first shared integration components, and presented the integration framework to the executive team.</p>

Fee Opt-In: Winning the Moment Donors Change Their Mind

An A/B test on the moment a donor edits their fee. The winning design took opt-in from 75% to 92%.

01Stakes

The Deceptive Win

A redesigned fee box on Raisely had just lifted opt-in from 75% to 82%, near the 90% benchmark. But the 12–20% of donors who clicked "Edit" mostly dragged the fee to the minimum or opted out. Awareness wasn't the problem. The opportunity was decision design: the few seconds after a donor decides to change something.

02Reframe

Reframing the Problem

The easy move was to ship the 82%. I argued to pause: the edit box dropped donors into an older fallback that nudged them toward the smallest contribution. The new question: how do we make the easiest path a fair one, without it ever feeling coercive? Donor trust is the product.

03Experiment

Designing the Decision, Not the Default

I designed three variants grounded in defaults, anchoring and soft-decline (a slider, toggle buttons and a segmented dropdown) and tested them against the existing control in Pendo. Each was built production-ready, so the winner could ship platform-wide the day the test closed.

The variants
Legacy fallbackRedesigned fallback
1Fragmented. The new edit box dropped donors into an older UI that looked and behaved differently.One consistent control, built production-ready so the winner could ship platform-wide.
2A slide to the smallest amount. The fallback quietly nudged donors toward the minimum.Anchored at a sensible default. "A little less" feels like a small step, not a cliff to zero.
3A blank-slate field. Donors had to invent an amount.Pre-framed choices in the toggle and dropdown variants, with a soft decline kept in reach.
04Prototyping

Prototyping With AI

Instead of static mocks, I built interactive prototypes in Cursor and MagicPatterns where PMs and engineers could change thresholds, defaults and fallback logic and watch the outcome. It cut alignment time and surfaced constraints before high-fidelity design. You can still try the version 1 prototype and the future-state concept.

05Hypothesis

The Hypothesis

Introducing a modernized fallback control when a donor clicks "Edit" will increase the average fee opt-in value while maintaining or improving the existing conversion rate.

06Result

What Happened

The Slider lifted fee opt-in from 75% to 92%, past the 90% benchmark, while conversion and average gift size held steady. It shipped as the fallback across the platform.

Improving how a user changes their decision can be just as effective as improving the default itself.

<p>An audit of the whole app turned up 55 findings: three different Back controls,
errors that showed a green check, four filled buttons on one screen. Writing
rules alone would not settle them; people needed to see the options. So I drew
12 open questions as small phone screens on one page, each with my
recommendation, and the team picked on their own time.</p>
<figure class="wide">
  <img src="/case-studies/goldito/ux-calls.jpg" alt="One of the 12 calls: three phone drawings for leaving a screen with something typed, keep a draft, ask first, or both, with both marked decided" />
  <figcaption>One of the 12 calls: what happens when you leave with something typed. The team chose both, drafts in long flows and a question in sheets.</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>Every pick became a dated rule in the team's design rulebook, and the developers
built them into the app the next day. One of them I changed myself. I had chosen
a Back control with the word "Back" beside the arrow, then saw it crowd the
sign-up progress bar. I switched to the arrow alone and logged why, because a
rule people can trust has to show its reasons.</p>

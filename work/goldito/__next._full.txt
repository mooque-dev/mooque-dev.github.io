<p>Goldito changed how I work. With one designer, a remote team and five weeks,
the loop that worked was:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Trial.</strong> Every open question becomes a page the team can tap, not a spec.</li>
<li><strong>Pick.</strong> Options drawn side by side with my recommendation; the team picks on its own time.</li>
<li><strong>Rule.</strong> Each pick is written down as a dated decision with its reason.</li>
<li><strong>Figma.</strong> A plugin captures every screen and flags what changed, so Figma never falls behind.</li>
<li><strong>Build.</strong> The developers, or I with an AI agent, build from the rule, and I review the pull request.</li>
</ol>
<p>What I'd keep from it:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A playable trial settles in an evening what a spec debates for a week.</strong> Twelve open questions, decided in one night.</li>
<li><strong>Write the reason next to the decision.</strong> It's what made it safe to reverse my own Back call when the evidence changed.</li>
<li><strong>Settle the patterns every screen uses first.</strong> Changing Back late touched the whole app.</li>
<li><strong>Treat design-to-code sync as a pipeline, not a chore.</strong> About 70 screens reach Figma in one run.</li>
<li><strong>AI lets one designer move at the team's speed, but the calls stay mine.</strong> I reviewed every screen, and 27 of the pull requests are mine.</li>
</ul>

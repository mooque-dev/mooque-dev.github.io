Orchid: A Design System Born From a Merger

Three products, 340+ components, and three teams who didn't choose each other. One shared system.

Role
Senior Product Designer

Aplos (now Velora)2025 to 2026

Problem
Three merged products, 340+ scattered components, and three teams who did not choose to work together.
Decision
I treated it as an adoption problem: earned dedicated engineering time with cross-department survey data, then replaced heavy governance with 48-hour async reviews.
Outcome
One shared library, adopted across all three products.
Principle
Governance should reduce friction, not create it.
01Stakes

The Situation

After the acquisition, three products with three visual languages had to become one. I saw early that this wasn't a design system problem. It was a culture problem: three teams with different histories had to choose to build on shared foundations.

02The pitch

Making the Case

Earlier design system efforts had died without engineering time. So before pitching, I surveyed five departments, letting leadership see the problem in their own teams' words.

It worked: we got two dedicated frontend engineers, the first time a design system had earned that.

03Before and after

How We Built It

An inventory found 340+ unique components across the three products. Workshops with each team, not presentations to them, distilled them into a small set of shared primitives built on tokens, with Storybook as the source of truth and Chromatic catching regressions on every pull request.

Before
After

I first built tight governance, and contribution proposals dropped to near zero. The formality was the problem, so I rebuilt around 48-hour async reviews and light templates. Proposals doubled.

04Adoption

Getting Adoption Without Authority

I had no authority over most of the people who needed Orchid, so adoption had to be earned: weekly office hours, pairing with engineers during migration, a migration guide per codebase, and bi-weekly stakeholder reviews. Teams adopted it because it was faster, not because it was required.

05Result

What Happened

Orchid was adopted across all three products and is now the foundation of the unified experience.

A design system nobody contributes to is a style guide with extra steps.

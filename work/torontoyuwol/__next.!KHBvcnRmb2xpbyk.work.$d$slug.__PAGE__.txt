<p>I started building small tools, each for one annoyance. Adoption was never
mandated.</p>
<p><strong>Scheduling and attendance.</strong> A shared calendar of practices and performances,
with attendance tracked per date, so you can see who's in the room at a glance.
People pick their team and their language; the whole thing runs in Korean and
English because the company does.</p>
<p><strong>A bilingual script reader.</strong> The reader keeps the Korean and English scripts
in one place. <a href="https://torontoyuwol-brown.vercel.app/demo.html">Try the script reader demo</a>.</p>
<figure>
  <img src="/case-studies/torontoyuwol/script-reader.jpg" alt="The script reader demo in a dark theme: scene 1 of a sample script, The Moonlight Laundromat, with one character's lines highlighted, a search box for the script, and a My lines control in the corner" />
  <figcaption>The script reader demo, on a sample script: one character's lines highlighted, a search box, and a My lines control</figcaption>
</figure>
<p><strong>Bill splitting.</strong> A small app splits a bill and keeps the math out of the
group chat.</p>
<p>The people using these tools were non-technical, and I saw them every week. They
told me directly what was broken, and I iterated weekly on that feedback. When
the stage manager walked over to say the attendance view confused her, that
told me more than an analytics dashboard would have.</p>

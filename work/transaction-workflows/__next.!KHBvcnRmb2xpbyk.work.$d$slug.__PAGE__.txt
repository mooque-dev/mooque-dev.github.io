<p>Four changes, each answering something the accountants showed us. Suggested categories became a platform rule I later wrote into Orchid's guidelines, and the new design <mark class="pen" data-note="the year-end surprise, gone">validates continuously</mark> instead of at report time.</p>
<div class="tablewrap"><table class="changes"><thead><tr><th></th><th>Before</th><th>After</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><span class="n">1</span></td><td data-label="Before"><b>One type per payment.</b> A gala ticket that was part donation, part value received had to be logged as several records.</td><td data-label="After"><b>One payment, split by purpose.</b> Donation, in-kind gift and value received live in a single transaction.</td></tr><tr><td><span class="n">2</span></td><td data-label="Before"><b>Categorizing by hand.</b> Every transaction started blank, and accountants kept a parallel spreadsheet.</td><td data-label="After"><b>Smart defaults, human override.</b> A suggested category from donor history, amount and fund, accepted in one click.</td></tr><tr><td><span class="n">3</span></td><td data-label="Before"><b>"Death by checkbox."</b> A flat table where batch edits meant ticking rows one at a time.</td><td data-label="After"><b>Batch by context.</b> Scope by date range, donor, fund or category, then act on the group.</td></tr><tr><td><span class="n">4</span></td><td data-label="Before"><b>Errors at report time.</b> A miscategorized transaction stayed invisible until the final report.</td><td data-label="After"><b>Continuous validation.</b> Each reporting period shows its health as you go.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

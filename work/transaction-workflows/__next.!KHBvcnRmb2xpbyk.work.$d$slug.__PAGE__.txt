<p>I sat with 8 nonprofit accountants while they reconciled. Every one kept a parallel spreadsheet. None could see a miscategorized transaction until the final report. One called batch editing "death by checkbox."</p>
<p>Every one of those payments also had to produce a correct tax receipt in three countries, and each draws the line differently:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Canada.</strong> When a donor gets something back, the receipt shows the total paid, the value of that advantage, and the eligible amount left over.</li>
<li><strong>The US.</strong> A payment over $75 that is partly a gift needs a written disclosure with a good-faith estimate of what the donor received.</li>
<li><strong>Australia.</strong> A payment at a fundraising event is deductible only if the benefit is minor: worth no more than 20% of the payment or $150, whichever is less.</li>
</ul>
<p>The required fields came from legal and compliance research. My job was to make getting them right the easy path.</p>

<p>The easy path was a one-off split screen for the loudest complaint. I pushed for extensibility instead, so the next capability wouldn't need a rewrite. Before designing, the PM, the engineering lead and I agreed on what we couldn't do: no backend refactor, no breaking the data model behind reporting. We shipped in phases.</p>
<figure>
  <img src="/case-studies/transaction-workflows/image-5.jpg" alt="Phased delivery plan, stakeholder alignment on constraints" />
  <figcaption>Phased delivery, without a backend refactor</figcaption>

<p>I made four changes, each answering something the accountants showed us. Suggested categories became a platform rule I later wrote into Orchid's guidelines. The new design also validates continuously, while people work.</p>
<div class="tablewrap"><table class="changes"><thead><tr><th></th><th>Before</th><th>After</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><span class="n">1</span></td><td data-label="Before"><b>One type per payment.</b> A gala ticket that was part donation, part value received had to be logged as several records.</td><td data-label="After"><b>One payment, split by purpose.</b> Donation, in-kind gift and value received live in a single transaction.</td></tr><tr><td><span class="n">2</span></td><td data-label="Before"><b>Categorizing by hand.</b> Every transaction started blank, and accountants kept a parallel spreadsheet.</td><td data-label="After"><b>Smart defaults, human override.</b> A suggested category from donor history, amount and fund, accepted in one click.</td></tr><tr><td><span class="n">3</span></td><td data-label="Before"><b>"Death by checkbox."</b> A flat table where batch edits meant ticking rows one at a time.</td><td data-label="After"><b>Batch by context.</b> Scope by date range, donor, fund or category, then act on the group.</td></tr><tr><td><span class="n">4</span></td><td data-label="Before"><b>Errors at report time.</b> A miscategorized transaction stayed invisible until the final report.</td><td data-label="After"><b>Continuous validation.</b> Each reporting period shows its health as you go.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
